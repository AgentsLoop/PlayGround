# Lantern Lake Dash

## Objective

Deliver **Lantern Lake Dash**, a polished small browser game for casual players. Let the player steer a tiny boat around a moonlit lake, collect drifting lanterns, avoid rocks, and reach the dock before the timer expires. Make the game immediately playable in a browser with keyboard and touch controls, clear feedback, restart flow, score, timer, win, loss, and recovery states.

## Quality bar

- Use a concrete visual reference: the calm, readable presentation of a minimalist arcade game such as *Mini Metro*, adapted for the lake-dash theme. Preserve the reference's strong visual hierarchy, simple interaction model, and readable game state while creating original artwork and identity.
- Research authoritative or representative game references before coding. Search screenshots and footage, select at least six high-signal visual anchors, download every selected anchor into `reference-assets/`, inspect the downloaded files directly, and record each source URL beside its local filename as provenance. Do not replace downloaded assets with Markdown image links.
- Inspect the real browser artifact with vision and direct interaction. Compare captures side by side or with a blind A/B review against the quality bar whenever practical.
- Deliver production-quality presentation, responsive layout, accessible contrast, operable keyboard and touch controls, pause or restart behavior, collision feedback, timer feedback, score feedback, win and loss states, and robust recovery from invalid or repeated input.

## Execution

- Have the lead choose the route and divide the work into independently judgeable pieces.
- Give each important piece a separate builder and a fresh-context critic or verifier. The verifier must inspect the actual artifact, identify the largest remaining gap, and send the work back for improvement.
- Sustain the work across as many days or sessions as needed. Keep the game playable while iterating.

## Verification

- Test the complete playable loop in the real browser at desktop and mobile-sized viewports.
- Verify movement, collection, hazards, timer expiry, scoring, win, loss, restart, touch input, keyboard input, resize behavior, and recovery after repeated actions.
- Record concrete evidence for behavior, visual quality, accessibility, and delivery. Fix the largest remaining gap and repeat the build, inspect, verify, and improve loop until the acceptance bar is met or the run stops with explicit evidence.

## Progress

Maintain a resumable live progress page with references, decisions, completed work, evidence, blockers, current gaps, and the next exact action so another agent can resume without losing context.
